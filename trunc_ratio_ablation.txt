RoPE Truncation Ratio Ablation Study
=====================================
Base = 570 (tuned for 256-position attention window)
500 training steps, batch_size=4, seq_len=4096

Run 1: Testing 0.40, 0.50, 0.60
--------------------------------
trunc=0.40  val_loss=5.3066
trunc=0.50  val_loss=5.7285
trunc=0.60  val_loss=6.0391

Run 2: Testing 0.00, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40
-------------------------------------
trunc=0.00  val_loss=6.2207
trunc=0.20  val_loss=5.6035
trunc=0.30  val_loss=5.4570  <- BEST
trunc=0.40  val_loss=5.8125

Combined Results (sorted by val loss):
======================================
trunc=0.30  val_loss=5.4570  <- OPTIMAL
trunc=0.40  val_loss=5.3066-5.8125 (variance)
trunc=0.50  val_loss=5.7285
trunc=0.20  val_loss=5.6035
trunc=0.00  val_loss=6.2207
trunc=0.60  val_loss=6.0391

Key Findings:
=============
1. No truncation (0.0) performs worst - confirming that zeroing
   the slowest-varying frequencies helps

2. The optimal truncation appears to be around 0.30-0.40
   (not the 0.50 used by upstream)

3. Too much truncation (0.60) also hurts performance

4. There's noticeable run-to-run variance (~0.3-0.5 val loss)
   which limits the precision of these short ablations

Interpretation:
===============
With base=570 and 256-position attention window:
- Frequencies span wavelengths from 2 positions to ~570 positions
- The highest frequencies (shortest wavelengths) are most useful
  for distinguishing nearby positions
- The lowest frequencies (longest wavelengths) don't complete
  even one cycle in the attention window, providing less signal
- Zeroing ~30% of the lowest frequencies appears optimal
- This is less aggressive than upstream's 50% truncation,
  possibly because our shorter base (570 vs 1024) already
  concentrates frequencies in the useful range

Recommendation:
===============
For this setup (base=570, 256 attention window):
- Use trunc_frac=0.30 instead of 0.50
- May need to re-tune base if changing truncation fraction
